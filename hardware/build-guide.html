SightLine

A small turret that quietly keeps an older family member in view at home. A radar sensor follows them as they move, and an iPhone rides on top. If they're out of view for too long, a caregiver's phone buzzes, and one tap starts a video call to see the room.

Radar does the tracking, so it works in the dark and through blankets. The iPhone's camera is only on during a FaceTime call, and the person sees who's calling on its screen.

How it works

One ear, a small wiggleThe radar faces straight ahead. Every second or so the head glances a little left, then a little right, and turns toward whichever side "heard" the person more strongly.
Follow, then searchThe pan-tilt servos keep the person centered. If the radar loses them for 2 seconds, the head sweeps side to side looking.
Alert, then lookStill nobody after 20 seconds? It sends a phone alert over WiFi. Tapping it FaceTimes the turret's iPhone, which answers by itself, so the caregiver can see and talk.

How the pieces go together

3D-printed build

The physical build order, from the box floor up to the phone. The full steps after this also cover loading the program, alerts and testing.

    Build it, step by step

    About 3 hours of hands-on work. Pick your version below; the steps and the 3D model change to match. Tick each step off as you go; your progress saves in this browser. Use Show in 3D to highlight the parts each step uses.

    1. Print the three parts

      ~5 h printing, start first

      The files are in your team repo under hardware/cad/stl/. Print in PLA, 0.2 mm layers, 20% infill, no supports. Put each part flat side down.

      • base_box.stl: the enclosure (about 3½ h)
      • base_lid.stl: the lid; the pan servo drops into its cutout (about 1 h)
      • radar_phone_head_single.stl: holds the radar facing straight ahead, with a backplate above it for the iPhone (about 1½ h). Print it standing on its flat base. Using two radars instead? Print radar_phone_head.stl and set RADARS to 2 in the program.

    2. Make the cardboard box and radar mount

      30 min

      You need: a sturdy shoebox (at least 7 cm tall), a cereal box or other stiff card, a craft knife, a ruler, a hot glue gun or double-sided tape, and something heavy (a power bank or a handful of coins).

      1. Servo hole. In the middle of the shoebox lid, draw a 41 × 20 mm rectangle and cut it out. Cut it slightly small, then shave it until the servo body just slides in.
      2. Wire hole. Next to the servo hole, cut a hole about the size of a coin for the radar and servo wires.
      3. Cable holes. In one end of the box, poke a hole for the power plug and a hole for the USB-C cable, both about 3 cm up from the bottom.
      4. Radar mount. Cut a card rectangle 40 mm wide × 50 mm tall. Fold a 15 mm tab along the bottom edge so it stands up like an L. The radar goes on the front of the upright part, facing straight ahead.
      5. Stiffen it. Run a line of hot glue along the inside of the fold so it stays upright. A wobbly mount makes the tracking jittery.
      6. Phone board. Cut thin plywood or very stiff card to 70 × 90 mm, plus a strip 44 × 17 mm. Glue the strip across the board 25 mm up from the bottom, sticking out forward, as a shelf the phone rests on.

      Want it sturdier? Use a small wooden board (about 15 × 10 cm) instead of the shoebox lid. Cut the same servo hole, then push 4 carriage bolts up through holes in the corners and add nuts so it stands on legs. The breadboard sits on the table underneath.

    3. Load the program onto the Feather

      20 min

      Do this before wiring anything, so you know the brain works. Plug the Feather in on its own: out of the breadboard, nothing attached, not resting on anything metal. Use a USB-C cable that carries data; many only charge.

      Keep pin 12 clear. If anything pulls the pin labeled 12 high while the board powers on, its memory runs at the wrong voltage and every upload fails with "MD5 of file does not match data in flash". Never put a wire in pin 12's breadboard row, now or later.

      First time on a Mac: install the USB driver. The Feather talks over USB through a WCH chip, and the Mac's built-in driver for it garbles uploads.

      1. On Adafruit's driver page, download CH341SER_MAC.ZIP. It must say MAC; CH343SER.zip is the Windows driver.
      2. Unzip it, open CH34xVCPDriver.dmg, and drag the CH34xVCPDriver app into Applications. Installing straight from the disk image doesn't work.
      3. Open CH34xVCPDriver from Applications and click Install.
      4. System Settings → General → Login Items & Extensions → Driver Extensions: switch CH34xVCPDriver on. Then unplug the Feather and plug it back in.

      Then in a terminal, run these one line at a time. The second line finds the Feather's port by itself:

      cd ~/github/hackathon-project/hardware/firmware/sightline
PORT=/dev/$(ls /dev | grep -m1 wchusbserial) && echo "Feather is on $PORT" || echo "Feather not found: check the driver, the cable, and replug it"
cp -n secrets.example.h secrets.h
arduino-cli compile --upload -p $PORT --fqbn esp32:esp32:adafruit_feather_esp32_v2 .
arduino-cli monitor -p $PORT -c baudrate=115200

      PORT only lasts in that terminal window, so in a new window run the first two lines again. If you only ever see /dev/cu.usbserial-… and never wchusbserial, the driver isn't switched on yet.

      Teammates without the tools can use the Arduino IDE instead: add the ESP32 boards, install the ESP32Servo and ld2410 libraries, pick Adafruit Feather ESP32 V2, then click Upload. Or just ask Claude in herdr to "flash the SightLine firmware".

      Check: the upload ends with "Hash of data verified", and the monitor prints lines like radar= 0 pan= 61 … SEARCHING WiFi=off. With no radar connected that's expected, and it means the program is running. Press the Feather's Reset button to also see the "Radar NOT found" startup line.

    4. Set up power on the breadboard

      20 min

      See these wires in 3D → The wiring model shows the exact breadboard hole for every wire.

      1. Drop one 1/4"-20 nut into the hex pocket in the box floor. The breadboard will hold it in.
      2. Peel the breadboard's backing and stick it in the box floor.
      3. Push the Feather into the breadboard so it straddles the middle gap, with its USB-C end facing the slot in the box wall.
      4. Push the Feather into the breadboard so it straddles the middle gap, with its USB-C end toward the cable hole you poked.
      5. Screw two wires into a female barrel adapter: red into +, black into −. Push it into the round hole in the box wall (hot glue it if loose).
      6. Screw two wires into a female barrel adapter: red into +, black into −. Hot glue it inside the box, right behind the power hole, so the plug can push in from outside.
      7. Red wire → the breadboard's red (+) rail. Black wire → the blue (−) rail.
      8. Jumper from the Feather's GND pin → the blue (−) rail. Every part must share this ground.
      9. Link the rails. A half-size breadboard has a red and a blue rail along both long edges, and the two edges aren't connected. Add a red jumper from one red (+) rail to the other, and a black jumper from one blue (−) rail to the other.
      Don't connect the red 5V rail to any Feather pin. The Feather gets its own power from USB-C (laptop while building, a phone charger for the demo). Always unplug the power supply before changing wires.

    5. Wire the two servos

      10 min

      See the servo wires in 3D → The wiring model shows the exact breadboard hole for every wire.

      Each servo has a 3-wire plug. Push male-to-male jumpers into it:

      • Black → blue (−) rail
      • Red → red (+) rail
      • Yellow → Feather pin 27 (pan servo) or pin 33 (tilt servo)

      The pan servo turns left and right; it's the one that sits in the lid. The tilt servo nods up and down.

    6. Center the servos, then build the pan-tilt neck

      40 min
      1. Plug in the Feather's USB-C and the 5V supply. The servos wiggle, then stop at center. Unplug both.
      2. Now assemble the Lynxmotion kit by its instructions. Because the servos are centered, attach the horns and brackets so the top bracket faces straight forward and level.
      3. Drop the pan servo into the lid's rectangular cutout and screw its four tabs to the lid with the kit's servo screws.
      4. Drop the pan servo into the hole in the shoebox lid so its tabs rest on top. Screw the tabs into the cardboard with the kit's servo screws. If they spin loose, put a bead of hot glue under each tab instead.
      Check: power both again. The head looks left, then right, nods down, and returns to facing forward.

    7. Wire the radar

      20 min

      Open the radar hookup model → It walks through the four wires one at a time, lighting up each sensor pin and breadboard hole.

      The radar has 5 pins. Read the labels printed on your board; the order differs between sellers. Use male-to-female jumpers (female end on the radar):

      Radar pinGoes to
      VCCred (+) rail
      GNDblue (−) rail
      TXFeather RX
      RXFeather TX
      OUTnot used

      TX always goes to the Feather's receive pin (RX), and RX to its transmit pin (TX). Crossing them like that is correct.

      Check: press the Feather's Reset button with the monitor open: it says "Radar OK". Wave a hand in front of the radar and the radar= number jumps up.

    8. Mount the radar and close up

      15 min
      1. Stick the radar on the front of the card mount with double-sided tape or a dab of hot glue on its back corners. The side with the printed antenna pattern faces out; keep glue off it.
      2. Glue or tape the mount's bottom tab onto the top bracket of the pan-tilt kit, so the radar faces straight forward, the same way as the phone will.
      3. Feed the radar and tilt-servo wires down through the wire hole, then put the lid on the box.
      4. Put the weight inside the box, in the corner farthest from the servo, so the box doesn't tip when the head turns. Tape the lid down at the corners.
      Keep metal away from the radar fronts. Cardboard, plastic and tape are fine because radar sees through them. Foil, a metal bracket or a phone in front of a radar blinds it.

    9. Mount the radar head and close up

      15 min
      1. Slide the radar down into the head's slot from the top. The side with the printed antenna pattern faces out, through the window; the chip and pins face back.
      2. Fix the head onto the top bracket with two M3 screws through the slots, or with foam tape. Its open windows face forward.
      3. Feed the radar and tilt-servo wires down through the round hole in the lid, then press the lid onto the box.
      4. Mount it: screw it onto a camera tripod, or push a carriage bolt up through a hole in a wooden board and screw the box onto it.

    10. Glue the case and mount the iPhone

      15 min + 1 h curing
      Take the phone out of the case first. Super glue fumes leave a white haze on glass and camera lenses, and a drip can seal a port or button. Put the phone back only after the glue has cured for at least an hour (a full day is best).
      1. Check your case. Super glue holds hard, stiff plastic cases well but barely sticks to soft, bendy silicone or rubber ones. For a soft case, use 3M VHB double-sided tape or E6000 glue instead.
      2. Glue the phone board upright behind the folded radar card, onto the top bracket, so its shelf sits just above the top of the radar. Hot glue works well here.
      3. Hold the empty case sideways (landscape), screen side facing forward, resting on the shelf and centered left to right. Mark where the backplate touches it. Keep glue well away from the camera opening.
      4. Scuff the marked area and the backplateand the board with sandpaper. Put small dots of gel super glue on the backplateboard, not one big puddle, then press the case on for 60 seconds.
      5. Once cured, snap the phone into the case. Run its Lightning cable down through the lid's wire hole, leaving a loose loop so the head can turn fully left and right without tugging it.

      The phone sits above the radar on purpose. Radar can't see through a phone, so never let it cover the radar's window.

      Check: power on. The self-test turns the head left and right and nods with the phone on board, without the servos buzzing or the head drooping. If it droops, the phone isn't centered front to back over the tilt joint.

    11. Turn on phone alerts

      10 min
      1. Install ntfy on your phone and subscribe to a hard-to-guess topic name, like sightline-team-7f3k.
      2. Open secrets.h and fill in your WiFi name, its password, and the same topic name.
      3. Upload again (the same compile --upload command as before).
      Hackathon WiFi often has a sign-in page, which the ESP32 can't get past. It also only joins 2.4 GHz networks. Use your phone's hotspot instead.
      Check: the monitor shows WiFi=on. Walk out of the room: the LED blinks slowly while it searches, and about 20 seconds later your phone buzzes. Walk back in to get a "back in view" message.

    12. Set up the live view (FaceTime)

      10 min

      On the turret's iPhone:

      1. Settings → Accessibility → Touch → Call Audio Routing → Auto-Answer Calls: on, set to 3 seconds. FaceTime calls now connect by themselves.
      2. Settings → Phone → Silence Unknown Callers: on. Save the caregiver as a contact so only they get auto-answered.
      3. Keep it plugged in with the volume up, and turn Low Power Mode off.

      Then in secrets.h, set FACETIME_TO to the turret iPhone's phone number or Apple ID email (like "+15551234567") and upload again.

      Ask first. Make sure the person being watched knows about the camera and agrees to it. Whenever a call is live, they see the caregiver's face on the screen.
      Check: walk out of view. When the alert arrives on the caregiver's iPhone, tap it. FaceTime calls the turret, it picks up after 3 seconds, and the caregiver sees the room.

    13. Tune it

      15 min

      The settings are at the top of sightline.ino, under "Tuning":

      • Slow to follow you? Raise WIGGLE_GAIN (try 0.3) or WIGGLE_MAX_STEP (try 10).
      • Twitchy? Raise DEADBAND (try 12) or lower WIGGLE_DEG (try 6).
      • Sees people who aren't there? Raise MIN_ENERGY (try 25).
      • Alerts too fast or too slow? Change ALERT_AFTER_MS. For a demo, 10000 means 10 seconds.
      • Phone steering jerky or slow? The motors follow the phone's aims with a small PID controller each (under "Website steering"). Jerky starts or stops: lower PAN_ACCEL / TILT_ACCEL. Slow to reach the aim: raise PAN_KP / TILT_KP a little (try 15). Trails behind someone walking: raise PAN_KI (try 30). Tilt struggling with the phone: lower TILT_ACCEL (try 1000).

      The head keeps gently wiggling while it follows someone; that's how it listens for which way they moved. Radar can see through thin walls and doors, so "out of sight" really means out of radar range (about 6 m) or out of its cone.

    14. Connect the web app (optional)

      20 min
      1. In Supabase, open SQL Editor, paste in hardware/supabase.sql and click Run. This creates an events table.
      2. Copy the Project URL and anon key into secrets.h, then upload again.
      3. Ask your first mate: "Build a caregiver dashboard on Vercel that shows the Supabase events table live, with big text for older eyes."

    If something's off

    "Radar NOT found" after wiring
    TX and RX are probably swapped, or VCC isn't on the 5V rail. Check the labels printed on the radar itself.
    Servos twitch or the Feather restarts
    The grounds aren't shared. The Feather GND must connect to the blue rail.
    "Feather not found", or no port shows up
    Try a different USB-C cable, since many are charge-only, and plug straight into the Mac, not a hub. If only /dev/cu.usbserial-… appears, switch the CH34xVCPDriver driver extension on.
    Upload fails with "MD5 of file does not match data in flash"
    Something is pulling pin 12 high. Take the Feather out of the breadboard, plug in only USB-C, and upload again. If it still fails, install the Mac USB driver from the upload step.
    Head points the wrong way when centered
    Take the servo horn off, rotate it one spline, and put it back on.
    Head droops or servos buzz with the phone on
    The phone's weight isn't over the tilt joint. Re-center it front to back, or make sure the shelf holds it close to the backplate.
    FaceTime doesn't pick up
    Check that Auto-Answer Calls is on, that the caregiver is saved as a contact on the turret phone, and that the turret phone is online.
    No phone alert
    Check that the monitor shows WiFi=on and that the topic in secrets.h exactly matches the one in the ntfy app.
